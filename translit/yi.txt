# Yiddish (Hebrew script) from Latin letters, as Yiddish is typed in Latin:
# YIVO's romanization, loosely (kh, sh, ts, zh, tsh; ey, ay, oy), or as
# English or German spell its sounds (ch, sch, tz, j, w). Yiddish writes
# its vowels: a and o are both א, as the wiki mostly spells them (the
# pointed אַ and אָ cost a little more), e is ע, i י, u ו, ey and ay יי, oy
# וי, and a word's first i, u, ey, ay or oy rides an alef (in: אין, oyf:
# אויף). Its words from Hebrew keep Hebrew's spelling, leaving vowels out,
# writing ח ת ש for kh, s and t (emes: אמת, khaver: חבר), and ו where
# Yiddish says e for Hebrew's o (sholem: שלום): ways at a cost, for the
# lexicon to find. m, n, kh, f and ts take their final forms at a word's
# end (fun: פון).
