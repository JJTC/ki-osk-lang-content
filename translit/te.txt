# Telugu (Telugu script) from Latin letters, as Telugu is typed in Latin.
# Telugu writes every vowel it speaks, so two consonants typed together
# are joined by the virama; a word's last m (and n) is the nasal sign
# (kalam: కలం), any other last consonant takes the virama. dh is థ as
# well as ధ (pradhamika: ప్రాథమిక), dd ద్ధ (paddathi: పద్ధతి), ai ాయి
# (mumbai: ముంబాయి), a last y ి (reddy: రెడ్డి), and e, b, g and k the
# vowel a consonant carries, భ, జ and ఖ, at a cost.
# English words, which the language writes in its script too, are typed
# as English spells them: c, ck, sch, o, er, y and tion have English
# ways too, at a cost.
# Its costs are fitted to how people romanized the Dakshina dataset's
# training words (CC BY-SA 4.0; make fit-translit, NOTICES.md), each
# held to the one first written here.
