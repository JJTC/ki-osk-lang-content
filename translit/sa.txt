# Sanskrit (Devanagari) from Latin letters, as Sanskrit is typed in Latin:
# loosely, as Hindi is (a long vowel often as a short one, ri for ऋ, sh for
# both श and ष), or with the capitals of Harvard-Kyoto and ITRANS for the
# letters plain Latin lacks (A I U long, T D N retroflex, Sh and S ष, M ं,
# H ः, R ऋ), each of which reads the plain letter too, for a capital that
# only starts a sentence.
# Unlike Hindi, Sanskrit speaks every inherent vowel: a word's last a is
# mostly that (rama: राम), and its last consonant keeps its virama nearly
# as often as its vowel (asmin: अस्मिन्; bharat: भारत); h at a word's end is
# the visarga (ramah: रामः), as is the h and the vowel before it said
# again, as Sanskrit is recited (ramaha, guruhu, harehe: रामः, गुरुः,
# हरेः), m there the anusvara as often as म् (ayam: अयं, aham: अहम्),
# and a nasal before a consonant of its row is the conjunct (shanti:
# शान्ति, pancha: पञ्च, anga: अङ्ग), seldom ं. ' is the avagraha
# (so'ham: सोऽहम्), which may be left out (sopi: सोऽपि), om alone is ॐ.
# Names from English are typed as English spells them (city: सिटी): c,
# ck, a last y and er have English ways too, at a cost. The lexicon
# chooses among them.
