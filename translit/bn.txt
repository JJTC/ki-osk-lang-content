# Bengali (Bengali script) from Latin letters, as Bengali is typed in
# Latin: a for আ (ami: আমি), o for the vowel a consonant carries (kotha:
# কথা) or for ো (bhalo: ভালো), t and d for ট and ড as well, s for শ, and
# the vowel between two consonants left out where it is not spoken.
# ৎ before a consonant as at a word's end (utsob: উৎসব), tty ত্য
# (ottyachar: অত্যাচার), tt ত্ব too (netritte: নেতৃত্বে), ddh ধ (maddhyome:
# মাধ্যমে), nch and nj ঞ্চ ঞ্জ (onchole: অঞ্চলে), and a hyphen typed
# before a suffix nothing (bangladesh-er: বাংলাদেশের). English words, which the language writes in its
# script too, are typed as English spells them: c, ck, sch, o, er, y and
# tion have English ways too, at a cost.
# Its costs are fitted to how people romanized the Dakshina dataset's
# training words (CC BY-SA 4.0; make fit-translit, NOTICES.md), each
# held to the one first written here.
