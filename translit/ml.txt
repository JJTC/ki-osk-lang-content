# Malayalam (Malayalam script) from Latin letters, as Malayalam is typed
# in Latin. Malayalam writes every vowel it speaks, so two consonants
# typed together are joined by the chandrakkala; a word's last n, l, r
# and ḷ are chillus (avan: അവൻ), its last m the nasal sign, any other last
# consonant takes the chandrakkala, and a last u is often only that
# (aanu: ആണ്). nt is ന്റ (ente: എന്റെ), mb and nch മ്പ and ഞ്ച (pattambi:
# പട്ടാമ്പി, panchayath: പഞ്ചായത്ത്), ri the vowel ൃ (krishnan: കൃഷ്ണൻ). Its
# doubled consonants are as often typed single (layipichu: ലയിപ്പിച്ചു,
# palakad: പാലക്കാട്), its long a as a, a last am as om (thadakom: തടാകം)
# and ആയി as aye (nannaye: നന്നായി). English words, which Malayalam writes
# in its own script, are typed as English spells them (meter: മീറ്റർ,
# school: സ്കൂൾ, india: ഇന്ത്യ): c, sch, a t as റ്റ, nd, ia and tion have
# English ways too, at a cost.
# Its costs are fitted to how people romanized the Dakshina dataset's
# training words (CC BY-SA 4.0; make fit-translit, NOTICES.md), each
# held to the one first written here.
